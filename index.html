Verify the Network

Your phone can help keep the world's networks honest.

Instead of trusting carriers to grade their own homework, a mesh of ordinary phones checks coverage in real time and logs every check on-chain. Run the Unity app as a licence operator on a phone you already own, or one sitting in a drawer, and earn a share of network revenue for it. A small, real enterprise you set up once and keep running.

See how it works Become an operator
  1. Step 01Get your operator codeFill in the short expression of interest form.
  2. Step 02Install the Unity appAndroid or iOS, everyday phone or an old one.
  3. Step 03Run itEnter your code, sign in, leave it running.
  4. Step 04Get rewardedPaid in UP tokens (1 UP = 1 USD).

No activation credits are required right now. Later, keeping the app running is expected to cost about US$2 a month (to be confirmed), taken from rewards. The app shows the current terms before you commit.

how a build runs

One project. All four modes.

Constructive leads here, but no project runs on it alone. Each one moves through the same four phases, one for each mode.

  1. 01 · Curious

    Plan it

    Understand what you're making and why. Pick a shape, learn what's inside before you trust it.

  2. 02 · Constructive

    Build it

    The parts, the steps or the setup. Ship a rough version first. It gets better once it exists.

  3. 03 · Caring

    Tend it

    Keep it healthy, repair before you replace, and give back what you learned so the next builder starts further along.

  4. 04 · Chill

    Live with it

    Use the thing. A tool that does one job and waits for you is a calm tool. That was the point all along.

why build

Momentum beats motivation.

You don't need scale to matter. Help one person. Fix one thing. Constructiveness is measured by contribution, not visibility.

Without it
Helplessness. Spectator mode.
Too much
Hustle addiction. Burnout.

Each mode stabilises the others. Curious opens, Caring grounds, Chill restores. See the cycle.

  • Done and rough beats perfect and imagined.You can't improve a thing that doesn't exist yet. Every guide here aims at a first working version, then refinement.
  • Shrink the task until starting feels almost too easy. Then start.Size is the enemy, not difficulty. Builds are broken into steps you can finish in one sitting.
  • Build in the open.Feedback early is cheap; feedback late is expensive. Plans are open, parts are standard, and what you learn goes back in.
  • Make, don't just consume.A life of only consuming is a life lived on someone else's defaults. Make the thing, write the thing, fix the thing.

01 · TRUST

Every trial runs on trust.

Participants protected. Data that is real. The person who checks it, site by site, is the Clinical Research Associate: the CRA.

QWhy should I care? Because your licence to operate rests on it.
02 · OVERLOAD

Eight systems. One person. Every week.

CTMS, EDC, eTMF, the site file, safety, IxRS, spreadsheets and email. The same fact is entered three to five times and reconciled by hand. Every hop is a chance for an error.

QIs this real? Yes, and it is structural: built into how the systems are (not) connected.
03 · HIDDEN RISK

Errors hide in the gaps.

Checks are sampled: five of 38 consent forms. A consent signed on a superseded version, an expired GCP certificate or a visit out of window is found at the next monitoring visit, weeks later.

QWhy hasn't this been fixed? Each system is fine on its own. The risk lives between them.
04 · CONSEQUENCE

And the risk lands on your desk.

An inspection finding. A delayed submission. A question from the regulator that you must answer. Inspection readiness becomes a project instead of a state.

QWhat does it cost? Findings, delay and credibility.
05 · THE TURN

Capture once. Check on arrival.

TrialThread runs one thread through the systems you already have. Each record is captured once, checked against protocol, regulation and SOP the moment it arrives, and routed to everyone who needs it. Nothing is ripped out.

QDo I replace my systems? No. They stay exactly where they are.
06 · COVERAGE

Eight AI observers check 100%. Not a sample.

Every consent form, report statement, visit window, training record and query, checked as it happens. The observers read; they never change anything. The signal a sample would miss is found the same day.

QCan AI be trusted? It checks. It never decides.
07 · JUDGEMENT

A named human judges, and signs.

The conductor reviews only what the observers raise. She confirms, regrades or dismisses each one, giving a reason, and signs with a passkey. She didn't do the work, so she can judge it.

QWho is accountable? A named person, with a verifiable signature.
08 · PROOF

Proof, on one page.

Every record, decision and signature is hashed and chained. Each month the board gets three answers on one page, and every number links to a signed record an inspector can verify without trusting us.

QWhat do you need from me? One study. Eight weeks.
01 · The systemic risk

It isn't the people. It's the gaps.

Big platforms serve the sponsor and site tools serve the site. The CRA works across all of them with no tool of their own, and six risks are built into the seams.

R1

Duplicate entry

Source → worksheet → EDC → the CRA's spreadsheet → monitoring report. Source data verification exists largely to catch errors the process itself introduced.

R2

No shared truth

CTMS, EDC, eTMF, the site file, safety and IxRS each hold part of the picture. Reconciliation is human.

R3

Shadow spreadsheets

Trackers for documents, deviations, actions, payments and training sit outside validated systems: no audit trail and no access control.

R4

Email as the record

Decisions, query answers and approvals live in inboxes, invisible to an inspector until asked.

R5

Late detection

An out-of-window visit, a superseded consent or an unqualified delegate is found weeks after it happens.

R6

Evidence after the fact

Inspection readiness is a project. Every inspection means reconstructing what happened from fragments.

02 · Why now

The regulators just moved the bar.

ICH E6(R3) re-centres Good Clinical Practice on quality by design, risk-proportionate oversight, data governance and fit-for-purpose computerised systems, including the ones the site uses. The spreadsheet layer can't evidence any of it.

03 · The product

One thread. Six moves.

A records-ingestion and workflow layer for clinical trial operations. It overlays the systems sponsors already mandate. It doesn't replace them.

01

Ingest once

Scanned source, eSource exports, delegation logs, CVs, certificates and findings all enter through one intake. Each is classified, extracted and hashed on arrival.

02

Check in bounds

A Rule → Standard → Policy engine checks every record against protocol, regulation and SOP: visit windows, consent versions, eligibility, expiry, delegation and training.

03

Flow to every role

The same verified record reaches the coordinator, investigator, CRA, PM, data manager, auditor and finance, each seeing only what their role permits.

04

Prove it

Every output is hashed into a ledger and every signature is a verifiable credential. Every decision is linked to the clause, section and source that justify it.

05

AI assists. Humans sign.

AI drafts the report, proposes the classification and pre-fills the query. A named human accepts, edits or rejects, and that choice is recorded.

06

Observe, don't do

Eight AI observers check 100% of the CRA work. A human conductor reviews what they raise, signs, and presents a verifiable picture to the board.

04 · Check in bounds

Rules that run at the door.

Three tiers, authored as data and linked to the clause each one implements. A breach produces a typed finding, never a silent failure. Watch a record arrive.

Tier 1

Rule

from the protocol
  • ·Visit 4 occurs Day 28 ± 3
  • ·Exclusion 7: eGFR < 45
  • ·ICF v3.0 required from 12 Mar
Tier 2

Standard

ICH GCP · 21 CFR · TGA · NHMRC
  • ·Consent before any study procedure
  • ·E-signature shows name, date/time and meaning
  • ·No delegated task without current training
Tier 3

Policy

sponsor · CRO · site SOPs
  • ·Trip report final within 10 working days
  • ·GCP training renewed every 3 years
  • ·Major deviations escalated to PM in 24 h

One breach, caught at the door: a consent signed on a superseded version becomes a typed finding the same day, linked to the rule it breaks. Without that, it waits for the next monitoring visit.

09 · Built for inspection

Designed to the regulation, clause by clause.

21 CFR Part 11 and ICH E6(R3) data governance are design constraints, not a validation afterthought. Each requirement maps to a feature.

11.10(a)

System validation

A risk-based validation package each release, with a summary for customers.

11.10(b)

Copies for inspection

Export any record with its full trail. A time-boxed inspector view.

11.10(c)

Protection & retention

Immutable versioning, anchored by hash, with retention set per jurisdiction.

11.10(d)

Limited access

Role-based access, study and site scoping, and blinding segregation.

11.10(e)

Audit trail

An append-only, hash-chained log of who, when, what and why.

11.10(f)(g)

Operational & authority checks

Sequence enforced (consent before procedures) and permission checked on every action.

11.10(i)

Training

Training records are credentials. Untrained users are blocked from delegated tasks.

11.50 · 11.70

Signature manifestation & linking

Name, time and meaning on every signature, which is bound to the record hash.

11.100 · 11.200

Unique signatures

One identity per person. Passkey plus PIN or biometric at the moment of signing.

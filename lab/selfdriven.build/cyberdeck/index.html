Project 01 · the idea behind the deck

The deck is the lesson, not the gadget.

A hand-built portable computer is a niche hobby. What it stands for (owning your tools, keeping your attention, making instead of only consuming) is for everyone living in the age of intelligence. You don't have to pick up a soldering iron to keep any of it.

A cyberdeck is the most literal version of a question worth asking about almost everything you use: Do I own this, or does it own me?

four values, four modes

The deck runs on the same four modes you do.

selfdriven.you is built on four modes. A cyberdeck happens to make each one visible in metal and plastic. Here's what the deck does, and what the same value looks like in a life with no workbench in it.

Curious01 / 04

Understand your tools

In the deck

The builder opens the case and learns every wire. Nothing inside is a mystery they're asked to simply trust.

In your life

You don't need the soldering iron, just the posture. Ask how the things you use actually work, and who decided they'd work that way. A sealed box is a quiet way of being managed; literacy is leverage.

Constructive02 / 04

Make, don't just consume

In the deck

It's assembled, not bought whole. Accountable to the builder's choices, not a vendor's roadmap or next forced update.

In your life

Prefer tools you can shape over tools that shape you. Make the thing, write the thing, fix the thing. A life of only consuming is a life lived on someone else's defaults.

Caring03 / 04

Build it to last, and to share

In the deck

Standard parts, open plans, repair over replace. Builders publish what they learned so the next person starts further along.

In your life

Choose durable over disposable, in objects, skills and relationships. Leave things better documented than you found them. Care is mostly just attention paid forward.

Chill04 / 04

Keep your attention

In the deck

There's no feed engineered to pull you back. The deck waits for you; it never competes for you. The constraint is the feature.

In your life

Pick tools that respect your attention instead of mining it. A thing that does one job won't spend your day for you. Calm is a design choice you're allowed to demand.

The deck doesn't make you self-driven. It just makes a self-driven mindset something you can see and hold.

The point
no workbench required

Even if you never build one.

Six small moves that carry every value of the deck, none of which need a single screw. Pick one this week.

Curious

Run a sealed-box audit

Name three tools you use daily. Could you explain how each works, or leave it if you had to? Notice which ones own you.

Constructive

Make one finishable thing

Something small you can complete this week: a page, a script, a shelf. Ship it rough. Consuming less and making a little changes the ratio.

Caring

Repair instead of replace

Fix one thing this month you'd normally bin: a jacket, a lamp, a habit. Durable is a choice you make over and over.

Chill

Keep one feed-free device

A notebook, an e-reader, an old phone wiped clean. One surface in your life with nothing engineered to pull you back.

Curious→Caring

Document one thing publicly

Write down something you figured out, where a stranger can find it. The whole maker world runs on people who do this.

Chill

Choose the tool you can turn off

When two options do the job, pick the one that lets you fully stop. Being able to switch it off is a feature worth paying for.
